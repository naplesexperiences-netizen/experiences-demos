It is not only embarrassing. Each episode costs an average of €1,200 and — worse — can damage your online reputation for months. The good news? In 95% of cases it is entirely avoidable, if you put the right measures in place.

What overbooking is, and why it really happens

Overbooking happens when you sell more rooms than you actually have for a given date. Technically it is simple; in practice it comes from four main causes:

  1. Slow synchronisation between portals — the booking arrives on Booking.com, but availability on Airbnb updates fifteen minutes later. In those fifteen minutes someone books the same room.
  2. Human error — a member of staff forgets to close availability on a channel after a phone booking.
  3. Deliberate overselling — some chains practise controlled overselling, counting on cancellations. A risky strategy for anyone without the experience to run it.
  4. Miscalculated no-show history — you overestimate how many guests will not turn up and accept more bookings than you should.
⚠️ A myth worth killing: "overbooking only happens to large properties". It does not. Four-room B&Bs have proportionally the same overbooking rate as hundred-room hotels, because they often run less sophisticated systems.

The seven mistakes that cause overbooking

Mistake 1: relying on manual updates

You tell yourself you check every morning. But overbooking arrives at 11pm on a Saturday, not at 9am on a Monday.

Mistake 2: a channel manager without certified XML

Some cheap channel managers use scraping instead of official XML connections. The result: delays of five to fifteen minutes on every update.

Mistake 3: selling on too many channels without control

More portals means more potential sources of overbooking if synchronisation is not perfect. Five well-synced channels beat twelve half-connected ones.

Mistake 4: not protecting your scarce rooms

Suites, junior suites, accessible rooms — the ones you only have one or two of — deserve special rules: a two-night minimum, no last-minute sales.

Mistake 5: ignoring seasonality

In high season the risk is four times higher. It calls for stricter rules.

Mistake 6: a PMS disconnected from the channel manager

If a direct booking from your website does not automatically update OTA availability, you are exposed.

Mistake 7: never reading the synchronisation logs

Channel managers record every update. If you never look at them, you will not know when something goes wrong.

This is the foundation. Without a modern channel manager with certified XML connections, every other measure limps.

  • Certified XML connections (not scraping) for Booking.com, Expedia, Airbnb and Agoda

Fix 2: a deliberate safety buffer

Even with the best channel manager there is always a theoretical margin of risk — two bookings in the same fraction of a second. The answer is a buffer: keep one or two rooms off the portals during critical periods.

Fix 3: automatic stop-sell rules

Configure your channel manager to close sales automatically when certain conditions are met:

  • When two or fewer rooms of a type remain, raise the rate by 25%
  • When one room of a type remains, close every channel except your own site
  • On high-risk dates — events, long weekends, summer Saturdays — set a two or three night minimum stay
  • Block same-day bookings after 10pm on the portals with the slowest sync
💡 A professional's trick: at demand peaks (New Year, mid-August, Easter), close the last two or three rooms on every portal 48 hours ahead and handle them only from reception or by phone. You cut the overbooking risk by 90% and still sell at your top rate.

A ten-point anti-overbooking checklist

Print it, pin it up in the office, go through it once a month:

  1. ✅ I have a channel manager with certified XML on every main channel
  2. ✅ My PMS has a two-way integration with it
  3. ✅ I have a safety buffer set for my peak season
  4. ✅ I have automatic stop-sell rules for scarce room types
  5. ✅ I have minimum-stay rules for critical periods
  6. ✅ I receive automatic alerts for synchronisation anomalies
  7. ✅ I read the sync logs at least once a week
  8. ✅ My team knows what to do when an overbooking happens — a written protocol
  9. ✅ I have relocation agreements with at least two partner hotels of the same standard
  10. ✅ I review every episode, even small ones, to understand the cause and prevent the next

What to do when it happens anyway

Even with every precaution it can still happen. Here is the emergency protocol:

  1. Tell the guest immediately — do not wait for check-in
  2. Apologise plainly and take responsibility
  3. Offer relocation to a property of higher standard, never lower
  4. Cover the transport and the price difference
  5. Add a voucher or discount for a future stay
  6. Follow up the next day with a personal phone call
  7. Ask for private feedback before the public review

Handled well, the emergency can turn a bad episode into a neutral or even positive review. Eight guests in ten who are looked after properly after an overbooking do not leave a negative review.

What a modern AI chatbot actually does

Forget the chatbots of 2018, with their buttons — "1 for prices, 2 for location, 3 to speak to an operator" — that frustrated people and were abandoned at the second click. An AI chatbot for hotels today is something else entirely.

A modern AI chatbot is a conversational assistant built on large language models — the same kind of technology behind ChatGPT, but trained on your property's information. It understands natural language, answers in more than 30 languages, handles complicated conversations, quotes live rates and, above all, closes the booking inside the chat, so the guest never has to leave the app they are writing in.

📌 In two lines: an AI chatbot for hotels is your digital front desk, talking to guests on WhatsApp, Instagram, Messenger and your website around the clock, and turning enquiries into direct bookings with no OTA commission.

The five moments when it makes you money

This is not theory. Here are the five situations where an AI chatbot is the difference between a lost booking and money in the bank.

1. The night, when you sleep and the world books

Between 10pm and 7am roughly 37% of enquiries arrive, mostly from markets in other time zones. Without a chatbot they sit unanswered for eight or ten hours, and the customer goes elsewhere in the meantime. With one, 82% of those conversations resolve on their own, and 19% become a confirmed booking before you open your eyes.

2. Enquiries in languages you do not speak

Your team answers in Italian and English. But if a message arrives in German at 11pm, what do you do? Run it through Google Translate and hope? An AI chatbot speaks more than 30 languages fluently, colloquialisms included. The result: 22–28% more business from non-English-speaking markets.

3. The direct booking, with no OTA commission

A guest writes on WhatsApp: "Do you have a double for the weekend of the 12th?" The chatbot queries your booking engine, offers three options with rates, and closes the booking with a payment link. You save the 15–18% commission you would have paid Booking.com for the same room.

4. Upselling, automatically

While the guest is booking, the chatbot offers: breakfast in the room? An airport transfer? A guided tour of Pompeii? A good one converts 11–16% of those offers, adding an average of €32 per booking.

5. Reviews after the stay

After check-out the chatbot sends a personal message, gathers feedback privately — catching complaints before they reach TripAdvisor — and invites satisfied guests to leave a public review. On average, 45% more positive reviews collected.

⚠️ Beware of fake AI: plenty of products sold as "AI chatbots" are static rules or decision trees underneath. To tell the difference, try this: write a long, rambling question such as "I read on your site that you do breakfast but my husband is coeliac and I don't know if you have gluten-free things because he had trouble in Naples last time". If the bot answers the substance, it is AI. If it offers you buttons marked "Breakfast / Allergies / Reviews", it is a rules-based bot in disguise.

Mistakes to avoid when choosing

Three mistakes we see constantly in people who buy a chatbot unprepared:

  1. Thinking that switching it on is enough. A chatbot without a carefully built knowledge base answers badly and damages your reputation. Expect 30 to 40 hours of initial content work.
  2. Not connecting it to the PMS. Without that link the chatbot informs but does not sell. You lose 70% of the value.
  3. Never reading the conversations. The real return comes from tuning during the first 60 days. Switch it on and forget it, and six months later you have the same product you had on day one.

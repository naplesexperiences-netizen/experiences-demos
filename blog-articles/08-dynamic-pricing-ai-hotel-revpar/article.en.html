What dynamic pricing is, without the jargon

Think of airline tickets. The same Naples–Milan flight costs €49 three months out and €289 the day before. Same aircraft, same route, same service. What changes is demand at the moment you buy.

Dynamic pricing applied to hotels is exactly that: your room rate changes in real time according to dozens of variables — current demand, local events, day of the week, lead time, what competitors are doing, the weather, the customer segment. There is no "high" or "low" price: there is the price that maximises revenue at that moment.

📌 In two lines: AI dynamic pricing is software that changes your room rates automatically, every hour, to maximise RevPAR — revenue per available room. It is not about lowering prices: it is about selling at the right value at the right time.

Six things an AI pricing tool weighs, and you do not

When you update rates by hand you consider two or three things: the season, the weekend, maybe an event. An AI tool weighs 40 to 120 variables at once. Here are the six that matter most.

1. Current demand in the market

The AI reads live search flows on the metasearch engines — Google Hotels, Trivago, Kayak — and sees whether demand for your destination is rising. If searches for "hotel Naples 12 September" have grown 40% in the past six hours, it raises rates before you notice.

2. Local events, including ones you do not know about

Concerts, conferences, trade fairs, ceremonies. The AI has databases covering thousands of local events. If there is a medical conference in Pompeii on 23 October, it knows six months before you do and adjusts pricing for those dates.

3. What competitors are charging

The AI checks the rates of the five to fifteen competing properties you have named, every hour. If your main competitor raises prices you know within 30 minutes and respond — or deliberately stay below them to win volume.

4. The weather forecast

Weather forecasts move tourism demand by 12–18%. If sunshine is forecast over Sorrento for the weekend of 19 May while Rome has rain, searches for the coast multiply. The AI knows ten days ahead.

5. Lead time

The rate curve has to respond to how many days remain. A room still free three days before check-in is worth less — or more, depending on the occupancy curve — than one sold 60 days out. The AI manages that curve with surgical precision.

6. Customer segment

The AI recognises whether the request comes from a leisure guest (weekend, family) or a business one (midweek, single). It adjusts the price and offers different restrictions: minimum stay, packages.

The three most common mistakes

  1. Not integrating it with the channel manager. The AI works out the optimal rate, but if it is not published automatically on every channel you lose 70% of the value. Without integration you are back to manual pricing.
  2. Not loading local events. The AI has international databases, but Italian local events — village festivals, patron saints' days, regional festivals — have to be taught to it. Without a local calendar it misses the most profitable dates.
  3. Emotional overrides. After two good weeks the revenue manager "has a feeling" and reverses the rates by hand. The AI works if you let it work. Override on data, never on instinct.

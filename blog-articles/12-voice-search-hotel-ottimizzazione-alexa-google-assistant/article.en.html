How a voice search really works

Drop the idea that voice search is "Google, but you talk". It is a different thing. The flow is this.

1. Intent recognition

Alexa or Google work out what the user actually wants. "Find me a hotel" is commercial intent; "what time is it in Naples" is informational. On commercial intent, the engine favours transactional results: booking engines, OTAs, Google Business Profile.

2. Natural language processing

The system extracts the key entities: type of property (hotel), location (near Vesuvius), constraints (with a pool, under €120). Each becomes a filter.

3. Knowledge graph and featured snippets

Google draws on its Knowledge Graph — Business Profile, schema markup, structured data — and on featured snippets. Alexa mostly pulls from Bing and Amazon partners. Siri from Google and Apple Maps.

4. The single-answer paradigm

The system picks one answer. It reads it aloud. That is all. There is no page two. There are no other results. There is one winning answer, and everything else has lost.

📌 In two lines: optimising for voice search means designing every page of your site to be "the single right answer" to a specific spoken question. No longer ranking in the top ten: being the answer.

The golden rule: optimise for featured snippets

Google reads aloud from featured snippets — the boxes at the top of results — in 70% of cases. Win the snippet and you win voice search for that query.

How do you win one? By answering the question as concisely and clearly as possible, in the first part of the page:

  • A direct answer of 40–60 words immediately under an H2 that restates the question
  • Bulleted or numbered lists after the answer
  • FAQPage schema where the page is organised as questions and answers
  • Simple language, short sentences, subject-verb-object

How to structure the site

Four technical layers. All necessary, none sufficient on its own.

1. LocalBusiness, Hotel and FAQPage schema (not optional)

Without schema markup you are invisible to voice. The minimum:

  • Hotel schema: name, address, phone, star rating, facilities (pool, parking, wifi, breakfast, pet-friendly), check-in and check-out, price range
  • LocalBusiness schema: opening hours, area served, GPS coordinates
  • FAQPage schema: for every strategic question with a short answer
  • Review schema: aggregateRating with score and review count

2. An FAQ section on every service page

Every page — home, rooms, restaurant, spa, location — needs an FAQ section with questions in natural language:

  • "Is the hotel pet friendly?"
  • "How far is Naples central station?"
  • "Is breakfast included?"
  • "Can I check in after 10pm?"

3. Microdata for address, phone and hours

Your name, address and phone must be identical everywhere: site, Google Business Profile, TripAdvisor, OTAs, social. A single discrepancy in a phone number is enough to lose the engines' confidence.

4. Page speed under two seconds

Google penalises slow sites for voice, because a voice user is impatient — driving, hands busy, wanting an answer now. Target: LCP under 2.5s, INP under 200ms.

Five things to do this week

You do not need a large budget or a rewrite of the site. Five targeted steps, doable in seven days, already put you in contention.

1. Add an FAQ section to every product and service page

At least six to eight questions per page, in natural language — "Can I…?", "Is there…?", "How far is…?" — with answers of 40 to 60 words.

2. Implement complete schema markup

Hotel, LocalBusiness, FAQPage and Review. Validate with Google's Rich Results Test before publishing. Bad schema does more harm than none.

3. Rewrite headings so they can be answered in a sentence

Old: "Deluxe rooms". New: "Our deluxe rooms in central Naples: what they include and what they cost". The heading should contain the answer to the likely question.

4. Complete your Google Business Profile

Recent photographs — at least 40 — a 750-character description rich in spoken-query language, up-to-date hours, and a Q&A section you populate yourself. Weekly posts help further.

5. Build location-intent pages

For example: "How to reach our hotel from Naples airport", "What to do near our hotel in 24 hours", "Hotels five minutes from Naples central station". These catch geographic spoken queries with very high intent.

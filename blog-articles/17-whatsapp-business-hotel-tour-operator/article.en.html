Business App or Business API: the difference that matters

This is where the most common mistake happens. Many properties switch on the free app, use it badly, conclude that "WhatsApp does not work" and go back to email. They are two different products with different uses.

WhatsApp Business App: free, manual, with a clear ceiling

It is the app you download from the store. Free, installed in ten minutes, offering a product catalogue, quick replies, an away message and labels for organising contacts. It runs on one main device with up to four linked devices.

It is perfectly fine for a B&B, a small hotel up to around 30 rooms, or a tour operator with fewer than 15 departures a week. Below 500 messages a month and with a single person answering, the API would be expensive over-engineering. The limit arrives when you need several people answering at once, when you want automatic messages triggered from the PMS, or when you need to track who answered what.

WhatsApp Business API: the channel becomes infrastructure

The API is not an app: it is programmatic access activated through a certified provider — Twilio, 360dialog, Sendbird and Callbell are among the most used in Italy. It gives you multiple operators with queues and assignment, trigger-based automation, two-way integration with your PMS, channel manager and CRM, pre-approved templates for outbound messages, and real reporting.

The cost has two parts: the provider's platform fee (roughly €39–150 a month for an average property) and a per-conversation charge billed by Meta.

This is not bureaucracy for its own sake: it is the mechanism that has kept WhatsApp free of spam, and why open rates remain at 98%. There are three categories, priced differently.

The critical point in that diagram is the handover: a bot that does not know when to pass over destroys more value than it creates. The working rule is simple: two failed automatic attempts, or any keyword touching price, availability or a complaint, and the conversation goes to a person.

The six automations that generate the most value

You do not need to automate everything. In accommodation, six flows cover more than 80% of the available value.

1. Booking confirmation and arrival instructions

Triggered automatically when the booking enters the PMS. It contains the summary, the location with a map link, check-in times and parking directions — which in Naples and on the Amalfi Coast is the number one question. It cuts "how do I get to you?" phone calls and reduces no-shows on direct bookings.

2. Pre-check-in with document collection

Sent 48 hours before arrival with a link to the registration form and document upload. The guest fills it in from their sofa, not standing at the desk with a suitcase in hand. It is the automation that frees the most minutes at reception.

3. Pre-stay upselling

Sent five to seven days before arrival. Airport transfers, room upgrades, dinner at the property, an excursion to Pompeii or by boat. The timing is ideal: the guest has already decided to come, is planning their days and is at the most receptive point of the cycle.

4. Assistance during the stay

Wifi password, breakfast times, extra towels, restaurant bookings, taxis. Service conversations, therefore free, replacing the internal phone and reducing trips to the desk.

5. Review request after check-out

Sent three or four hours after departure, while the memory is fresh and the guest is travelling with their phone in hand. The response rate on WhatsApp is three to five times that of the same request by email.

6. Seasonal reactivation of returning guests

Sent 10 or 11 months after the previous stay, before the guest starts searching Booking again. It is the highest-margin conversation you can have: no commission, no acquisition cost, just a nine-cent marketing template.

WhatsApp is not a silo: fitting it into the rest of the stack

The structural mistake is treating WhatsApp as a separate inbox, on a company phone where only one person knows what has been said. If the conversation does not reach the CRM, the value evaporates as soon as that person goes on holiday.

The sensible minimum integration has three points. The PMS is the source of triggers: booking created, changed, imminent, closed. The channel manager supplies real availability, so when a customer asks "do you have a double from the 12th to the 15th?" the answer is verified rather than "I'll let you know". The CRM holds the history: every conversation enriches the guest profile, so at the next enquiry the operator already knows this is a returning guest who took the transfer last year.

On top of those three, an AI chatbot handles the first level around the clock — the repetitive questions, the night-time ones, the ones in languages nobody on the team speaks. The bot does not replace the operator: it filters the noise and leaves them the conversations that matter.

💡 If you can only switch on one, switch on pre-check-in. It is the highest-return automation for a hotel: it cuts reception time at the worst moment of the day, reduces document transcription errors and — a detail almost nobody uses — opens the upsell conversation naturally. A guest who has just uploaded their documents on WhatsApp is one message away from "shall we book your airport transfer?".

This is the story — a real one, in detail, with figures — of how they got there. If you run a small agency, work as a self-employed guide or are building a tour operation, read to the end. Most of it transfers.

The agency (we will call it "TourNap" for discretion) had been trading since 2019. It specialised in walking tours of the historic centre, excursions to the Royal Palace of Caserta and boat trips to Capri. Three partners, all with a decade of experience as guides.

The problem was not the quality of the product — TourNap had excellent reviews from everyone who had tried it. The problem was that nobody could find them. Growth was stuck on a floor of word of mouth they could not climb off.

📌 The real problem: 73% of international travellers book tours online, before they travel. If you are not on the right channels, you simply do not exist for them.

What finally convinced them

They had resisted "going digital" for months. Three familiar objections:

  1. "We have no marketing budget"
  2. "The platforms take too much commission"
  3. "Word of mouth still works, why change?"

The turning point came when they discovered that a direct competitor — founded after them, with less experienced guides — was turning over eight times as much. The secret? They were everywhere online.

They called Experiences Srl for a free audit. After 90 minutes of analysis the plan was clear. They said yes.

Step 1: a mobile-first website (days 1–21)

The existing site was a 2019 brochure: slow, not responsive, no booking system, poor photography. We rewrote it from scratch around these priorities.

What the new site had

  • Mobile-first: 78% of tourism traffic comes from phones
  • Under two seconds to load, with Core Web Vitals in the green
  • Bilingual Italian and English from the start, with room for German and French later
  • A built-in booking engine with online payment (Stripe and PayPal)
  • A page for each tour with professional photos, a map, FAQs and embedded reviews

Optimising the platform listings

Signing up is not enough. Each tour listing on an OTA is a small SEO page in its own right. For every tour:

  • A title built around the main keywords
  • A description of 1,500–2,000 characters, structured in points
  • Ten to fifteen high-resolution professional photographs
  • A detailed itinerary with timings
  • Highlights as a list — what makes this tour different
  • FAQs written around what people actually search for
  • Clear policies: cancellation, meeting point, accessibility

Step 4: an AI chatbot for round-the-clock bookings (days 60–75)

Forty-one per cent of enquiries arrived outside office hours. In plain terms: they were losing potential bookings every evening, every night, every weekend.

We put a multilingual AI chatbot on the site and on WhatsApp Business, able to:

  • Answer in Italian, English and Spanish
  • Know every tour, its price, its times and its meeting point
  • Handle the FAQs: cancellation policy, accessibility, children, the language of the tour
  • Check availability in real time
  • Take the visitor all the way to a completed booking
  • Hand over to the owner on WhatsApp only when a request is genuinely complex

What the chatbot did in its first 60 days

  • 340 conversations handled
  • 187 enquiries resolved on their own
  • 47 bookings generated
  • 92% satisfaction after the conversation
  • Average response time: 2 seconds, against six hours before
💡 The telling detail: 31% of the bookings the chatbot generated arrive between 9pm and 2am. That night window is mostly American and Asian travellers planning from home.

What you can do too: the checklist

There is nothing magic in TourNap's path. Any small agency or tour operator can repeat it. Here it is in eight points.

  1. Audit where you are now. What do you turn over? Where do guests come from? What is your conversion rate? Without a baseline you cannot tell whether anything is working.
  2. A mobile-first site with booking built in. This is not optional. Without it you lose seven visitors in ten. Typical one-off investment: €3,500–6,000.
  3. Go live on three to five tour OTAs. Start with GetYourGuide and Viator. Add Airbnb Experiences and Musement in the following months.
  4. Structured local SEO. Google Business Profile, content, schema markup. See the dedicated guide.
  5. An AI chatbot for round-the-clock enquiries. Especially if your market is international. Cost: €80–200 a month, with an excellent return.
  6. An automatic review system. Email or WhatsApp after the tour, with a direct link. Target: at least five to ten new reviews a month.
  7. A channel manager for tours. Once you pass 50 bookings a month across several platforms you need one. See the channel manager guide for tour operators.
  8. Monthly analysis. Measure everything. What works? What does not? Adjust as you go, not at year end.
⚠️ A common mistake: "I will do the website first, then think about the rest." Wrong. All eight should start in parallel during the first 60 to 90 days. Waiting to perfect one thing before beginning the next slows everything down.

In closing: digital does not replace, it amplifies

TourNap already had everything: passion, expertise, quality. What was missing was the amplifier that would let the world hear about it. Digital does not make you good if you are not: it makes you findable if you already are.

If you run a small agency or tour operation and you recognise yourself in TourNap's starting point, the path is clear, tested and repeatable. You do not have to be big to reach good numbers. You have to be present in the right places.

GetYourGuide: strengths, weaknesses, commission

GetYourGuide is Europe's leading platform for tours and experiences. Founded in Germany in 2009, it now handles over 60,000 activities in 170 countries and 75 million bookings a year.

How it works

  1. You register as a supplier (verification and onboarding take two to four weeks)
  2. You build your tour listings following their guidelines, which are strict
  3. You set availability, prices and restrictions
  4. GetYourGuide brings customers, handles payments and support
  5. You run the tour and are paid monthly

Commission and costs

  • Standard: 20–25% of bookings
  • "Originals" (premium): 30%, but with more visibility and marketing
  • No fixed monthly fee
  • Payment at 60 days net

Strengths ✅

  • A high-quality international audience (Europe, USA, UK)
  • Invests heavily in SEO and advertising, which brings you traffic
  • A very strong mobile app — 60% of bookings
  • Dedicated support, with a real account manager
  • A rigorous review system, which raises the bar for your tours
  • Assisted dynamic pricing

Weaknesses ❌

  • High commission (20–30%)
  • Long, selective onboarding — they turn down 40% of applications
  • Strict quality standards for photos, descriptions and operations
  • Heavy dependency: if they drop you, you lose a key channel
  • Low visibility for new tours until the first reviews arrive
📌 When GetYourGuide is the right fit: you are a guide or agency with genuinely high quality, you want access to the international market, and you have time to do the onboarding properly.

Viator (TripAdvisor Experiences)

Viator is the TripAdvisor group's tours platform: over 300,000 experiences across 1,500 destinations, strongest in English-speaking markets — the USA, UK and Australia.

How it works

  1. Sign up through Viator Supplier — faster than GetYourGuide, one to two weeks
  2. Your listings are synced automatically with TripAdvisor
  3. The same logic applies for availability, pricing and bookings

Commission and costs

  • Standard: 25% of bookings
  • Payment: after the tour runs, on a 7–14 day cycle
  • No fixed fee

Strengths ✅

  • Two channels in one: Viator plus TripAdvisor from a single profile
  • A strong English-speaking audience (USA, Canada, UK, Australia)
  • Simpler onboarding than GetYourGuide
  • Good presence in mainstream markets
  • Automatic syncing of TripAdvisor reviews

Weaknesses ❌

  • High commission (25%)
  • Very strong internal competition — many similar tours
  • An opaque algorithm: it is hard to tell why some tours rank better than others
  • Support in English, with limited Italian
  • A less modern interface than GetYourGuide
📌 When Viator is the right fit: your tours appeal to an American or English-speaking audience, you already have good TripAdvisor reviews, and you want an additional channel with quicker onboarding.

Airbnb Experiences: who it suits

Airbnb Experiences is Airbnb's experiences arm. More selective, focused on authentic, local activities rather than tourism in the classic sense.

How it works

  1. You apply through Airbnb — a competitive process; roughly 15% of applicants are accepted
  2. A quality check by video call
  3. The experience has to be genuinely distinctive and rooted in the place
  4. Publishing and management happen through the Airbnb app

Commission and costs

  • Standard: 20% commission
  • No fixed fee
  • Payment after the experience runs

Strengths ✅

  • The lowest commission of the tour OTAs
  • A young audience (25–40) that is willing to spend
  • Fits the Airbnb context — the trip is already booked
  • "Local" experiences carry enormous perceived value
  • An excellent app and a very good booking experience

Weaknesses ❌

  • Highly selective onboarding, with a high rejection rate
  • Not suited to mainstream tours — coaches, classic group outings
  • Lower volume than GetYourGuide or Viator
  • Hard to scale beyond a certain size
  • The discovery algorithm does not always favour newcomers
📌 When Airbnb Experiences is the right fit: you are a guide with something genuinely personal — a cooking workshop, a street-art tour with a local artist, a craft experience. It is not the channel for a generic historic-centre walk.

Your own site: when it makes sense, and what it costs

Your own site with a booking engine is the most underrated and most profitable channel. No commission. Your customer. Your data. Your brand.

How it works

  1. Build a mobile-first site with a booking engine
  2. Integrate a payment gateway (Stripe, PayPal, Nexi)
  3. SEO-optimised pages for every tour
  4. An availability system, which can connect to a channel manager
  5. Direct marketing: SEO, social, email, advertising

Typical costs

  • Initial setup: €3,500–8,000 for a site plus a professional booking engine
  • Running costs: €40–150 a month for hosting, booking engine and plugins
  • Payment fees: 1.5–2.5% with Stripe or PayPal
  • Marketing: variable — SEO investment, optional advertising

Strengths ✅

  • No OTA commission
  • The customer is yours: email, data, the ability to market to them again
  • Free pricing, your own rules
  • Cross-selling and upselling — tour plus transfer plus lunch
  • Brand building that compounds
  • No dependence on someone else's algorithm

Weaknesses ❌

  • You have to bring the traffic yourself: SEO, social, advertising
  • A meaningful upfront investment
  • SEO results take six to twelve months
  • You handle payments, customer service and disputes
  • International travellers will not stumble across you

Running several channels without losing your mind

If you sell on four or five channels at once, managing availability by hand is impossible and will sooner or later give you an overbooking or a pricing conflict.

The answer is a channel manager built for tours, which is a different thing from a hotel one. The main options in 2026:

  • Bókun (owned by TripAdvisor) — complete, and free up to four direct bookings a month
  • Rezdy — widely used globally, good for mid-sized operators
  • FareHarbor — US-focused, with an excellent interface
  • Trekksoft — European, with good support
  • TourCMS — UK-based, for more structured operators

In closing: the question is not where, but in what proportion

In 2026, selling tours on a single platform is short-sighted. GetYourGuide brings international volume. Viator opens the English-speaking market. Airbnb Experiences attracts a specific premium audience. Your own site is the asset that grows in value, pays no commission and gives you the customer data.

The winning strategy is not to choose one: it is to build a balanced mix, run it with a channel manager, and steadily grow your own site's share. Whoever works this out before their competitors wins the decade.

Sixty-one per cent of travellers under 35 choose their destination by watching a video on social, before they ever open Booking. If your property does not appear at that stage, you are not losing a booking: you never enter the shortlist. By the time the guest reaches the OTA they have already decided where to go and often which place to sleep in. You are competing on price in a race that was settled two weeks earlier.

The shift: social is no longer a shop window

For years the logic was: the website sells, social displays. Today it is the other way round. Instagram and TikTok are visual search engines: people type "things to do on the Amalfi Coast" or "hotel with a pool in Sorrento" inside the app, not into Google. And the platform returns videos, not blue links.

Every piece of content therefore needs a search intent inside it: on-screen text, caption and spoken words are all indexed. A Reel titled "Three places ten minutes from the hotel where there is never a queue" is not a nice piece of content, it is an answer to a query that keeps circulating for months.

The second consequence is economic: here reach is bought with time instead of budget. TikTok shows content to people who do not follow you, which no other owned channel does: it is the one place where a fourteen-room property reaches 200,000 people without advertising.

📌 The working rule: produce once, publish twice. The same vertical goes to TikTok for discovery and to Instagram Reels for consideration, with different captions. Anyone producing separate content for the two platforms gives up within six weeks.

The minimum sustainable plan: the 40/30/20/10 rule

Hotel profiles do not die from a shortage of ideas, but from the lack of a repeatable format. Four posts a week is the right target: below that you build no rhythm, above it you cannot keep going without a dedicated person. With two hours of filming and editing, four pieces are achievable.

  • 40% place and region. Not your room: Vesuvius at dawn, lemons picked by hand, the beach at seven in the morning. This is the content that travels: nobody saves a photograph of a hotel room, they save a place they want to be.
  • 30% the guest experience. Breakfast on the terrace, the detail of the Vietri ceramics, the sound of the coffee machine. Proof that what you promise actually exists.
  • 20% behind the scenes and people. Staff setting up the dining room, the mozzarella supplier five kilometres away, the cook kneading dough. It has the highest comment rate and it is the only thing nobody can copy from you.
  • 10% a direct offer. One commercial post in every ten: a rate, a package, a last-minute deal. It works because it is rare.

The 90/10 split between value and selling is not ethics, it is mechanics: the platforms distribute according to watch time, and nothing shortens it like a post that sells.

Guest content: the material you do not have to produce

Every week your guests shoot dozens of videos at your property, with an advantage your own content will never have: they are credible, because you did not make them. In 95% of cases they disappear within 24 hours into somebody's Stories, never touching your profile.

How to encourage it

Guest content does not happen by accident, it is designed. Four things are needed: a deliberately photogenic corner — a terrace, a staircase with bougainvillea, a breakfast plated to be photographed before it is eaten; a short property hashtag, written in the room, on the menu and in your email signature; a real incentive, an aperitif or a late check-out for anyone who tags you; and a QR code in the room, because when a guest wants to tag you they do not want to go searching.

How to get usage rights

This is where the most expensive mistake lives. Resharing gives you no usage rights. Resharing a Story you are tagged in is tolerated by the platform, but it does not authorise you to use that content on your website, in a paid campaign or on Booking. You need written permission: three lines in a DM — who you are, which content, where and for how long you will use it — with an explicit reply ("yes, I agree") and a screenshot kept. For advertising, a release by email is better.

How to reuse it

Authorised content has four lives: the gallery on your website; Meta campaigns, where it costs 30–50% less per click than a professional photograph; your Booking or Google Business listing; and pre-stay emails, where it raises the take-up of extras. No production, an hour a month to manage.

Working with creators: how to choose properly

Collaborations are the most effective shortcut to discovery and the fastest way to burn three thousand euros. The difference lies in the selection criteria, which are almost always wrong because they look at follower counts.

The data that matters is different. Engagement rate: below 2% the profile is inflated or exhausted; between 4% and 8% the audience is active. And above all the geographic audience: a creator with 80,000 followers who are 70% Brazilian is useless to an Amalfi Coast hotel selling into the Italian, German and British markets. Always ask for the country and age insights: anyone working seriously sends them straight away.

Third criterion, size. Micro-creators between 5,000 and 30,000 followers convert better than the large accounts: they have a real relationship with their audience and their recommendation carries the weight of a friend's. For the budget of one macro creator you engage four and spread the risk.

Payment model Strengths Weaknesses Cost range (Italy)
Barter / hosting Low marginal cost, fills the low season Little control over timing and quality, many requests 2–3 nights (real cost €80 – €200)
Fixed fee plus hosting Deliverables and usage rights in a contract A certain outlay even if the content underperforms €300 – €1,500 per micro-creator

Macro-creator (100k+) Immediate reach, visibility effect Low engagement, scattered audience €2,000 – €8,000 per campaign

Three things in writing avoid 100% of the problems: the number and type of deliverables (one Reel, three Stories, five raw photographs), the publication window, and usage rights for 12 months, advertising included. Without that last point you pay for content you can only look at.

⚠️ Collaborations must be disclosed. Always. In Italy the competition authority requires transparency in commercial communication: any content arising from an agreement must carry #adv or an equivalent disclosure clearly, at the start of the caption and not buried among twenty hashtags. No exceptions: this applies to pure barter too, where the only payment is the stay. Responsibility is shared and penalties reach the business as well: put it in the agreement as a clause and check the post the same day.

What to measure, and what to ignore

The most-quoted metric is the one that matters least: likes measure a gesture that costs nothing. Saves and shares measure intention — someone who saves a hotel video is planning a trip, someone who shares it is persuading another person to come — and they are the signals the platforms weight most heavily: optimising for saves is optimising for reach.

Then you move down the funnel. Clicks on the link in bio are the first monetisable number. Bookings attributed through UTM parameters are the truth: without tracking on the landing page, everything else is opinion. Finally, the figure that ends the discussion with the owners: cost per social booking against cost per OTA booking. If a social booking costs €38 and the same one from Booking costs 18% of €630 — €113 — the conversation is over.

If you have two hours a week, do not spread them across five fillers: put them into one strong piece — scout the location, wait for the right light, frame it deliberately, edit it properly. Distribution here has an extreme long tail: almost all the value comes from the few pieces that break through, and five mediocre posts never add up to one good Reel.

The 90-day plan

Days 1–30 — Foundations. Rewrite the bio with location, category and a clear benefit. Create the highlights: rooms, the area, breakfast, how to get here, reviews. Build the landing page connected to the booking engine, with UTMs on every link. Define the property hashtag, print the QR code for the rooms, identify the three photogenic corners. Then start: four pieces a week, TikTok open.

Days 31–60 — Volume and guest content. Keep the rhythm and read the data: which two formats generate the most saves. Set up the guest content process — written permission, an archive, reuse on the site and in emails. Approach eight to ten suitable micro-creators and close the first two collaborations in low season, with a written agreement and a disclosure clause.

Days 61–90 — Amplification and measurement. Move the budget to the two winning formats and put advertising behind the organic content that already performs: it costs less per click because it is validated. Close the other two collaborations for high season and set up monthly reporting on the seven KPIs.

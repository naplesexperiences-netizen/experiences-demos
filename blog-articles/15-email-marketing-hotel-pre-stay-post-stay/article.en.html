Sixty-eight per cent of people who start a booking on your site abandon it before confirming. A single automatic email sent 45 minutes after they leave recovers between 12% and 18% of them. Across 300 abandonments a year that is 40 extra bookings: direct, commission-free, and without a cent spent on advertising. Yet most hotels in Campania do not have a single sequence running.

Email marketing for hotels is not "sending a Christmas newsletter". It is a system of three automatic sequences working on the database you already own: people who visited the booking engine and did not finish, people who booked and have not arrived yet, and people who have just left. Three moments, three different commercial objectives, all measurable in euros.

Why email beats every other channel for a hotel

The difference is not aesthetic preference, it is economic structure. On Meta and Google you rent access to an audience: stop paying and the traffic disappears. With email you own the list. Every address collected is an asset that keeps producing revenue for years at almost no marginal cost.

The second point is commission. A recovered booking on a €180-a-night room for three nights is €540 of full revenue. The same booking arriving from an OTA at 18% leaves you €443. The difference, €97, is the margin that funds ten years of your email tool subscription.

The three sequences every property needs

You do not need twenty automations. You need three, built well and left to run. Each catches a different moment in the guest's life cycle and has a distinct commercial objective.

  • Sequence 1 — Abandoned booking recovery. Objective: convert someone who has already been on the booking engine. The most profitable of all, because it works on warm, qualified demand.
  • Sequence 2 — Pre-stay. Objective: raise the average spend with upselling and ancillaries before arrival, and reduce enquiries to the front desk.
  • Sequence 3 — Post-stay. Objective: collect reviews and turn the guest into a returner, the customer who costs nothing to acquire.

Sequence 1 — Recovering the abandoned booking

Someone who reaches the payment page and does not confirm is not browsing: they have already chosen dates, room and rate. They stopped for a mundane reason — a phone call, a doubt about cancellation, the urge to compare the price on Booking. Three emails across 72 hours recover a substantial share of these people.

Email 1 — 45 minutes after abandonment

The most important of the whole architecture. It should be helpful, not commercial: no discount, no pressure. Summarise the dates, room and price, and offer a link back to the basket already filled in. A plain subject line: "Your room for 14–17 June is still available". Conversion here runs at 12–18%.

Email 2 — 24 hours later

Change the lever: from logistical convenience to reassurance. Free cancellation, best price guaranteed on the official site, a direct WhatsApp contact with reception. This is where the honest comparison with the OTA belongs: the same price, but with priority upgrade and late check-out included where available. Expected conversion: 5–8%.

Email 3 — 72 hours later

The last call, with real scarcity, not invented: how many rooms remain for those dates. Only here, and only if the margin allows, can you introduce a light incentive — breakfast included, or 5% off — never an aggressive discount that erodes rate parity. Conversion: 3–5%.

⚠️ The recovery sequence only works if the booking engine captures the email before the payment step. If your engine asks for the address only at the last stage, you are losing the data of precisely the people who abandon. That is the first thing to check, before even choosing a tool.

Sequence 2 — Pre-stay: upselling and preparing the stay

On average 45 days pass between confirmation and arrival. In that window the guest is in the most favourable emotional state: they have already made the hard decision and are now imagining the holiday. It is the point of highest willingness to spend in the whole cycle, and most hotels leave it completely empty.

Seven days before — the experience email

Not a service email but a curated catalogue: airport transfer, sea-view upgrade, a tour of Pompeii or Capri, dinner on the terrace. Present it as "get your stay ready", not as a sale. Typical overall take-up: 18–25% of guests buy at least one extra.

Two days before — logistics and one last offer

Check-in times, how to get there, parking, the weather forecast. At the end, just two high-converting offers: transfer and late check-out. The simplification is deliberate — two days from departure, the guest has no appetite for choosing between ten options.

Arrival day — the welcome message

The name of the person on reception, a direct WhatsApp number, an expected arrival time to confirm with one click. No selling. This email cuts phone calls to the front desk and opens the channel you will use throughout the stay.

Sequence 3 — Post-stay: reviews and loyalty

A guest who has just left has the most vivid memory and the highest willingness to engage. Every day that passes without contacting them loses you both the review and the chance of a return.

24 hours after — the review request

A personal thank you and one link. The rule that doubles results: if the stay had known problems, the first email asks for private feedback; the public link to Google or TripAdvisor goes only to those who respond positively. This is not filtering reviews, it is routing the conversation to the right place. The response rate typically goes from 6–8% to 15–22%.

Seven days after — content and referral

No direct selling. Your best photographs, a guide to the places the guest did not have time to see, and a referral code to pass to friends and family. Its job is to stay in mind during the week they tell everyone about the holiday.

Three months after — the seasonal return offer

Here you sell. An offer built around the opposite season to their stay — someone who came in August receives a proposal for the spring break, with a "returning guest" rate and a limited booking window. Typical conversion 4–7%, at zero acquisition cost.

The minimum segmentation that actually works

You do not need twenty segments. Four are enough, built from data your PMS already holds:

  • Business against leisure. Inferable from length of stay and days of the week. Business guests get late check-out and transfers; leisure guests get experiences and dinners. Sending the wrong email to the wrong segment is the fastest way to be ignored.
  • First stay against returning. You do not explain to a returning guest where the property is: you acknowledge their status and give them a dedicated rate.
  • Nationality and language. At an Amalfi Coast hotel the mix is typically 40–60% international. A pre-stay sequence in English converts nearly twice as well as the same one in Italian for English-speaking guests.
  • Season of stay. Determines what to offer in the three-month email and over what window.

💡 If time is short, start with language and first-stay against returning alone. They capture 80% of the relevance gain for 20% of the configuration work.

⚠️ GDPR — the distinction you need to know. Operational pre-stay and post-stay emails (confirmation, logistics, check-in) are transactional: the legal basis is performance of the contract and they do not require marketing consent. Commercial offers, the newsletter and the three-month return email are promotional and require explicit consent, collected with an unticked checkbox, preferably double opt-in, with proof of collection retained and an unsubscribe link in every send. Mixing the two in the same automation is the mistake that exposes you to a penalty: keep them on separate lists and flows.

Setting it up in 30 days

Week 1 — Foundations. Check that the booking engine captures the email before payment. Choose the tool and connect it. Configure domain authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC): without it half your emails land in spam and everything else is pointless.

Week 2 — Sequence 1. Write and switch on the three abandonment recovery emails. This is the sequence with the fastest return: it goes first and starts producing within days.

Week 3 — Sequence 2. Build the upsell catalogue with prices and margins defined, set up the three pre-stay emails and how extras are paid for. Translate it all into English.

Week 4 — Sequence 3 and measurement. Switch on the post-stay emails, set the minimum segmentation, configure revenue attribution by email, and fix a monthly review of the numbers.

💡 If you implement one thing from this article, implement the 45-minute abandonment email. It is the single highest-return automation available to a property: a few hours of setup, no discounting, no effect on rate parity, and it works on customers who had already chosen you.

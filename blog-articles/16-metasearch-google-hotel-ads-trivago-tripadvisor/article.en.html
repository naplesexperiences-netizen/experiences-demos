When a customer searches your hotel's name on Google, the first thing they see is not your website: it is a box of rates. If Booking is in there at €140 and your official site is not, you have just paid 18% commission on a customer who was already looking for you by name. That is not a customer the OTA brought you: it is a customer the OTA intercepted in the last metre. Metasearch exists to take that metre back.

It is the highest-returning form of advertising available to an independent property, because it does not buy cold demand: it buys visibility at the exact moment someone has already decided where they want to sleep and is only choosing where to buy. Yet it remains the least used channel among Italian hotels under 100 rooms, often for a simple reason: metasearch gets confused with an OTA.

What metasearch is, and what it is not

A metasearch engine does not sell rooms. It compares: it collects your property's rates from every source publishing them — Booking, Expedia, your own site — and shows them side by side. When the user clicks your direct rate, they leave the metasearch and land on your booking engine.

The difference matters on three levels. The customer is yours: data, email and consent stay with you. The relationship is yours: you can upsell, run pre-stay, build loyalty. And you control the cost: you pay a click or a commission you decide, not a percentage imposed by contract.

What metasearch does not do is generate new demand, and it does not replace Google Ads or social. It does one thing, very well: it converts into a direct booking the demand already heading for your property that today ends up at the OTA by default.

The three pricing models

The cost model is not a contractual detail: it decides who carries the risk of the campaign, you or the platform.

  • CPC — you pay per click. You pay for every visit that reaches your booking engine, converting or not. The risk is entirely yours, but so is the control: you can push the markets that convert best and reach a very low cost per booking. It does require a booking engine that converts, otherwise you are funding traffic that bounces.
  • Commission CPA — you pay only on a booking. Typically 10–15% of the stay value, charged only when the booking happens. Almost no risk, and still less than an OTA, which takes 15–22% and the customer. It is the right starting point for anyone who has never done metasearch.
  • Fixed CPA. A locked percentage, no auction, visibility managed by the algorithm. Volume is less controllable but the cost is predictable to the cent: suitable for anyone who wants a certain budget line and no daily management.

The rule as you mature: start on commission CPA to build history and learn how well your own site actually converts. After three to six months, if the cost per booking on CPA is consistently above what CPC would give you, move to CPC on your best markets and leave CPA on the rest. A hybrid model is the most common configuration among properties achieving the highest returns.

Google Hotel Ads in detail: the main channel

Google Hotel Ads is the rate box that appears on your hotel's panel in Google Search, Google Maps and the travel section. It concentrates the large majority of metasearch traffic and holds the hottest moment of the whole funnel: the search for your name.

Getting started: you need a connectivity partner

You do not connect to Google directly. You need a connectivity partner — typically your channel manager or booking engine — sending Google rates and availability in real time. Octorate, SiteMinder, D-EDGE and The Hotels Network are among the most common partners for Italian properties; most modern booking engines offer the connection as a module.

The first question to ask your supplier is not "how much does it cost" but "are you already a certified Google connectivity partner?". If the answer is no, the cost of changing supplier belongs in the budget for the metasearch project, because without the connection there is no starting point.

Free Booking Links against paid campaigns

Since 2021 Google has also shown direct booking links free of charge, beneath the paid positions. These are the Free Booking Links: if your booking engine is connected, your official rate appears in the box at no cost. Paid campaigns buy the higher position and a far larger share of impressions, but basic presence is free.

Google Business Profile matters here

The rate box lives inside your Google Business Profile. Up-to-date photographs, correct hours, the right category, recent reviews and replies to them are not an image exercise: they raise the click-through rate on the rate box and therefore lower your cost per booking at the same spend. Five conditions for not burning budget

Metasearch is unforgiving: it amplifies whatever is downstream. If the site converts badly, the campaign simply buys more failures. Five conditions must be met before switching on a paid campaign.

  • Rate parity at least matching the OTA. If your direct price is higher than Booking's in the same box, the user sees the comparison and picks the other. You are paying to demonstrate that you are worse value. Parity is the minimum; a small advantage is better, even just breakfast included and stated in the box.
  • A mobile booking engine under 2.5 seconds. More than 70% of metasearch clicks come from phones. Every second beyond the threshold costs a double-digit share of conversion — on traffic you have already paid for.
  • Availability always current. You need a channel manager syncing in real time. A rate shown in the box and unavailable on the site produces a guaranteed abandonment and, if it repeats, a visibility penalty from the platform.
  • Conversion tracking configured. Tracking on the booking engine, the booking value passed through, attribution verified. Without the monetary value in the platform, the algorithm optimises blind and you do not know what you are buying.
  • Enough budget to leave the learning phase. Below 20 to 30 conversions a month the algorithm has too little data to optimise. One properly funded channel beats four underfunded ones.

A 45-day activation roadmap

Days 1–10 — Diagnosis. Check rate parity across every channel, test the booking engine's speed on mobile, review the Google Business Profile, confirm your supplier is a certified connectivity partner.

Days 11–20 — Technical foundations. Switch on the rate connection, publish the Free Booking Links, configure conversion tracking with monetary value, and run an end-to-end test of a real booking from the box through to the PMS.

Days 21–35 — Measure at zero cost. Two weeks of free presence only: how many impressions, how many clicks, how many bookings. That is the baseline you size the budget against, and meanwhile you correct any rate or availability mismatches.

Days 36–45 — The paid campaign. Start on commission CPA in your main markets, with a budget sized to your property, a weekly review of the first 30 days, and bids differentiated by market as soon as the data allows.

⚠️ Do not start paid campaigns before fixing rate parity and booking engine speed. With direct more expensive than the OTA, or an engine that takes five seconds, you are buying traffic that does not convert: you pay the click, the user compares, picks Booking, and you have funded the intermediary's commission. Both checks cost an afternoon and are worth more than any later campaign optimisation.

💡 Before raising the budget, always compare the cost per booking against the equivalent OTA commission. While you stay under 10% of the stay value you are gaining on every booking you move across: that threshold tells you when to push and when to stop, far better than the return multiple in the abstract.
